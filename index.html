VectorLLM: Language-Controllable Autonomous Driving via Vectorized Scene Grounding

A unified vector-language interface that represents map geometry and agent motion as discrete tokens for grounded reasoning, trajectory generation, and language-controllable autonomous driving.

01

Abstract

Natural language provides a flexible interface for humans to interact with autonomous vehicles. However, the effectiveness of this interaction relies on a driving model’s language grounding and controllability. Recent works have leveraged vision-language models (VLMs) as backbones but are still limited in grounding driving scenes with language. We present VectorLLM, a vectorized paradigm that grounds vectorized driving scenes with pretrained large language models (LLMs). The vectorized agent motions and map elements are represented as discrete tokens and learned to align with language in the LLM’s latent space. This approach directly incorporates driving information into LLMs to utilize pretrained LLM capabilities, e.g., chain-of-thought reasoning and language generation, to reason about and plan in a vectorized driving scene. We conduct extensive experiments with the Waymo Open Motion dataset and nuScenes dataset across multiple tasks, including trajectory prediction, question answering, and controllability under counterfactual scenes and instructions. We show that VectorLLM can leverage low-level driving information to generate trajectories and outperform existing LLMs/VLMs in WOMD-Reasoning and nuScenes-QA, demonstrating improved grounding. More importantly, VectorLLM demonstrates superior controllability compared to alternative models when responding to changes in scenes and counterfactual language instructions, offering an effective language interface for controllable driving interactions.

02

Overview

[Overview figure]

Grounding language directly in vectorized driving scenes

VectorLLM discretizes road geometry and agent motion into vector tokens and serializes them with language prompts in one sequence. Fine-tuning aligns the vector and language tokens in the LLM’s latent space, enabling one model to answer driving questions, reason about traffic context, generate trajectories, and respond to counterfactual scene changes or language instructions.

03

Method / System Design

[Architecture diagram]

Vectorization and tokenization

Static map elements and dynamic agent histories are transformed into the ego frame and represented as ordered two-dimensional displacement vectors. Continuous vectors are discretized using a 1,024-token scene vocabulary built with k-disks clustering. Semantic marker and delimiter tokens identify maps, ego motion, and other agents, while beam-search assignment reduces reconstruction drift along road polylines.

Expanded LLM vocabulary and grounded reasoning

VectorLLM expands a pretrained decoder-only LLM’s input embeddings and output head with vector and special tokens. It uses no separate map encoder, motion encoder, or projection layer. Chain-of-thought supervision selects relevant scene elements before producing an answer or trajectory; trajectory targets use goal conditioning and bidirectional attention for parallel decoding.

04

Training and Generation Process

[Process animation or sequence]

Training uses two stages with all model parameters trainable. Stage I performs next-token prediction over serialized vectorized scenes so the pretrained LLM learns map and motion structures as token sequences. Stage II jointly fine-tunes question answering, goal prediction, and trajectory generation. At inference time, the same serialized context supports language answers, intermediate goals, and trajectories; trajectory tokens are mapped back to displacement centroids and accumulated into metric coordinates.

05

Benchmarks & Tasks

Datasets: Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) and nuScenes.

Tasks: trajectory prediction, question answering, scene-edit controllability, and instruction controllability.

Grounding benchmarks: WOMD-Reasoning and nuScenes-QA.

Input settings: HD-map vectors and a perception-based VectorLLM+MapTR variant using vectorized maps predicted from multiview images.

06

Results

Language grounding: VectorLLM reaches 61.0% Acc1.0 and 0.804 ROUGE on WOMD-Reasoning, and 63.5% overall accuracy on nuScenes-QA.

Counterfactual control: It achieves a 62.5% overall goal hit rate on WOMD scene edits and a 75.4% average goal hit rate under nuScenes language instructions.

Trajectory prediction: On WOMD, VectorLLM reports 0.92 minADE, 0.25 miss rate, and 0.34 mAP. On nuScenes, it reports 0.39 L2 error and 0.17 collision rate.

08

Design Choices, Ablations & Limitations

Full VectorLLM: 0.92 minADE, 2.06 minFDE, 61.0% QA Acc1.0, and 0.804 ROUGE.

Corrupted chain of thought: 1.19 minADE, 2.54 minFDE, 53.7% QA Acc1.0, and 0.715 ROUGE.

Without chain of thought: 1.31 minADE, 2.75 minFDE, 51.2% QA Acc1.0, and 0.712 ROUGE.

The paper identifies four main limitations: VectorLLM is not jointly trained with raw visual perception; QA can struggle to localize temporal boundaries; trajectory errors may accumulate in fast-moving scenes; and large counterfactual deviations can be resisted by learned behavioral priors.
